The key is to understand why Inorder Traversal produces values in ascending order in a BST.


1. What is a Binary Search Tree (BST)?
A BST follows these rules:
- Left subtree: contains values smaller than the root.
- Right subtree: contains values larger than the root.
- Both left and right subtrees follow the same rule.

          8
        /   \
       4     12
      / \   /  \
     2   6 10  14



Look at the root, 8.
- Its left child is 4, which is smaller than 8.
- Its right child is 12, which is larger than 8.
- Under 4, the values 2 and 6 are smaller and larger than 4, respectively.
- Under 12, the values 10 and 14 are smaller and larger than 12, respectively.
Notice that 10 is greater than 8, but it belongs in the left subtree of 12. A BST rule applies to the entire subtree, not just the direct children.


2. What is Inorder Traversal?
Inorder traversal means:
Left → Root → Right
For every node, you do these three things in this order:
1. Traverse its entire left subtree.
2. Visit the node itself.
3. Traverse its entire right subtree.
Let's dry-run the example.
Step 1: Start at root 8
We cannot visit 8 immediately because inorder says to visit the left subtree first.
Go to 4.
Step 2: At node 4
Again, go left first, so we visit 2.
Node 2 has no left child, so visit 2. It has no right child either, so return to 4.
Our output is now:
[2]
Step 3: Finish node 4
We have finished its left subtree, so now visit 4.
Output:
[2, 4]
Next, traverse the right subtree of 4, which contains 6. Visit 6.
Output:
[2, 4, 6]
Step 4: Return to root 8
The entire left subtree of 8 is finished. Now visit 8.
Output:
[2, 4, 6, 8]
Step 5: Traverse the right subtree
Go to 12. Its left child is 10, so visit 10 first, then 12, and finally 14.
Final output:
Inorder traversal result
2 → 4 → 6 → 8 → 10 → 12 → 14
The values are in ascending order, from smallest to largest.



3. Why does this always work for a BST?
The reason is the BST property:
- Every value in the left subtree is smaller than the current node.
- The current node is smaller than every value in its right subtree.
Inorder processes all the smaller values first, then the current node, then all the larger values.
Because the same rule holds recursively inside every subtree, the entire output is sorted.



Remember: 

Inorder traversal does not sort an arbitrary binary tree. It produces ascending order because the tree is a valid BST.
